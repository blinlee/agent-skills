# Modeling Method

This is the semantic core. Use it after intake, full-corpus semantic reading, noise filtering, deduplication, extraction, classification, linking, and currentness review.

Do not model from a handful of memorable excerpts. The model should explain the corpus, including late sections, weak signals, exceptions, and internal contradictions.

## 1. Perspective Flip

Ask what the source author or system must believe for a rule to make sense.

Questions:

- What problem is this rule trying to solve?
- What failure does it prevent?
- What does the author treat as dangerous, valuable, or decisive?
- What is fixed in the worldview?

## 2. Experience Model

Infer the repeated situation behind the rule.

Capture:

- recurring environment
- constraints
- common failure modes
- feedback loops
- examples that shaped the rule

## 3. Mechanism Model

Explain why the rule works.

For finance, this may be liquidity, sentiment, incentives, reflexivity, positioning, or market structure.
For product, it may be user behavior, adoption friction, organizational incentives, or workflow cost.
For project work, it may be coordination, authority, trust, dependency, or feedback latency.

## 4. Narrative Reconstruction

Build a transferable doctrine from linked concepts.

Do:

- preserve tensions
- state boundaries
- separate principles from tactics
- keep evidence close
- explain whether contradictions coexist, bound each other, refine each other, supersede each other, or remain unresolved
- separate source claims from agent corrections or supplements

Do not:

- turn messy evidence into fake certainty
- write inspirational slogans before modeling mechanisms
- erase minority cases
- claim a clean doctrine when coverage gaps remain
- silently modernize old source claims without audit notes

## 5. Anchor Design

Create an anchor only after the principle and mechanism are clear.

Good anchors are:

- short
- memorable
- faithful to the evidence
- attached to a principle, mechanism, boundary, and sources

Bad anchors are:

- generic
- motivational but unsupported
- shorter than the idea deserves
- disconnected from evidence

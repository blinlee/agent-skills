# Product Review Notes

The team keeps comparing features by visible UI, but customers hire the product to reduce coordination cost. A feature that looks advanced may be irrelevant if it does not shorten the workflow.

Principle candidate: name features by the job they do, not by vendor terminology.

Anti-pattern: counting feature parity without depth. A shallow integration can increase support cost while looking complete in a comparison table.

Decision gate: before building a feature, identify the user role, the job, the switching trigger, and the evidence that the pain is real.

Open tension: speed matters, but rushing taxonomy creates future confusion.
